## Chartink Quick Scan (3-year high + volume)
Scan, generated by Chartink's "Magic Filters" from plain English: `Daily Close > 1 day ago Max(750, Daily High)` AND `Daily Volume > Daily SMA(Volume, 50) × 2`, F&O segment. Run 2026-11-19 (IST morning), on data for 2026-11-18.

| Symbol | Notes (looks like a real breakout?) |
|---|---|
| NAVINFLUOR | Close 10,278 (+4.72%) vs 3-year high 10,165, which was set on 2026-11-13, only **3 bars earlier**. A **continuation high**, not a base breakout. The stock has been making new highs all November (11, 12 Nov too). |

**Differences found vs Kanso's definitions**
- **Lookback:** Chartink 750 bars vs Kanso 756 (3 × 252). No difference here.
- **Volume average:** Chartink's `SMA(Volume, 50)` *includes* today's bar, while Kanso excludes it (§5.3). Including today makes the ratio a little lower (3.60× vs 3.80× on 2026-11-11), so surges look slightly weaker.
- **"Today" vs "fresh":** Chartink lists every stock where the rule is true *today*, which is Kanso's "All qualifying" mode. It has no fresh-signal view and no "true since".
- **Data freshness:** Chartink already had 2026-11-18. Yahoo's daily history ended at 2026-11-17 when checked about 4.5 hours after midnight IST. **Kanso's nightly ingest must not assume Yahoo has today's bar right after the close.** The ingest needs a "latest available date" check, and the scanner's `AsOf` must use the benchmark's last bar (already in the plan), not the calendar date.

**UX notes**
- Plain-English → rule ("Magic Filters") worked first try and showed the rule as editable blocks. That's a strong idea for Kanso's Quick Scan in a later version.
- Its "Backtest history" chart shows how many stocks matched on each of the last 9 months, coloured by sector. A cheap, useful view of *when* breakouts cluster.
- The page loaded with leftover example filters that silently OR'ed with the new rule ("passes any 1"). Easy to get wrong results, so Kanso's builder should start empty or clearly marked.

The order cost more than the available cash, so TradingView silently dropped it. The two junk trades (#4: 8 shares, #7: 1 share, entry = exit on the same day, −0.6%) are artifacts of the same problem. Fixed in the script by sizing at 90%.

**Re-run at 90% sizing:** 13 trades (12 closed plus 1 open since 2026-07-15 @ 7,260 ✅). The junk trades are gone. **Four real trades reappeared** that 100% sizing had silently dropped: 2006-09-05 (−13.5%), 2006-11-15 (−9.2%), 2015-08-11 (+1.7%, which replaced the 2015-08-20 −8.6% trade) and 2020-04-13 (−12.5%). The 2007 trade now enters a week earlier (2007-05-23 @ 200, not 2007-05-30 @ 250) and makes +88.4% instead of +50.7%.

Two losses exceed the 8% stop (−13.5% in Sep 2006, −12.5% in Jul 2020). A **gap through the stop** means the price opened below the stop level, so the exit filled at the worse open. Kanso models this the same way (§5.4).

## vectorbt (Nifty 50, defaults)
Run 2026-11-20 with vectorbt 1.2.1. Universe = **today's** Nifty 50 (niftyindices CSV) + DIVISLAB, Yahoo data. Rule: daily, 3-year high + volume ≥ 2× + RS > 0 + ≤ 8% extended. Exits: 8% stop, weekly close < 30-week SMA. Costs 0.2%/side + 0.1% slippage. Window starts 2007-09-17, when Yahoo's ^NSEI history begins.

**`v0/backtest_slots.py`** (Kanso-style portfolio: max 10 positions, equity/10 sizing, RS ranking):

| | System | Nifty 50 buy & hold |
|---|---|---|
| CAGR (19.2 y) | **10.2%** | 9.5% |
| Max drawdown | **−27%** | −60% |
| ₹10 lakh became | ₹64.9 lakh | ₹57.4 lakh |

- Trade level (every signal, single-stock mode): **321 trades, 38% win rate, avg win +41.5%, avg loss −7.5%, expectancy +11.1% per trade**.
- Portfolio: 216 of 323 trades taken (the rest found all slots full), 5.0 positions on average, invested on 83% of days.
- **Caveat: survivorship bias.** Today's Nifty 50 is full of past winners. TRENT (a top-5 trade, entered 2023) joined the index later *because* it rose. DIVISLAB, which has since been removed from Nifty 50, wasn't in the list at all, so I added it by hand. The real edge over Nifty is probably smaller than +0.7%/year. The **drawdown** advantage (−27% vs −60%, mostly 2008) is the more robust finding.

**Bugs found in my own V0 scripts (important for the V1 benchmark):**
1. `size_type="targetpercent"` isn't supported by `from_signals`, and vectorbt **has no "max positions" setting**. The first script (`backtest_vbt.py`) held up to **31 positions** at once. That confirms the spec's choice: Kanso's portfolio simulation is its own code, and the vectorbt benchmark compares **single-stock mode** only (§8).
2. **Stops checked on the close only.** Without `open/high/low`, vectorbt tests stops against the close. DIVISLAB **2020-07-02** gapped down to open at ₹2,100, below the ₹2,196 stop, then closed at ₹2,196.6, **20 paise above it**. With close-only checks the trade survived and showed +41.9% instead of −12.4%. **The V1 benchmark tool must pass open/high/low** (added to spec changes below).

**DIVISLAB, vectorbt (fixed) vs TradingView run 2:**
- **All entry dates match.** TradingView also has 2006–2007 trades, because its Nifty history is longer.
- **Trend exits come one day earlier in vectorbt.** That's the known Pine one-day lag.
- **Stops:** 2016-11-09 and 2020-07-02 match exactly.
- **Gapped stops:** on 2020-03-12, vectorbt fills at the stop price (₹1,919) while TradingView fills at the open (₹1,861), which is the expected allowed difference from §8.
- **⚠️ One unexplained difference:** the 2025 stop exits on 2025-08-08 @ ₹5,936 in vectorbt vs 2025-08-07 @ ₹6,053 in TradingView, and our hand calculation said 2025-08-06. Three tools gave three dates. Investigate during V1 Task 13, where the spec's §5.4 decides which is right.

### Experiments: 11 variations, same data (`v0/experiments.py`)
Today's Nifty 50 + DIVISLAB, 2007-09-17 → 2026-11-20. Nifty 50 buy & hold: CAGR 9.5%, max DD −60%.

| Variation | Trades | Win | Avg win | Avg loss | Exp/trade | Portfolio CAGR | Max DD |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Baseline (V1 defaults)** | 321 | 38% | +41.5% | −7.5% | +11.1% | 10.2% | −27% |
| No filters (3y high only) | 447 | 38% | +39.5% | −7.5% | +10.4% | 11.1% | −26% |
| Without volume filter | 427 | 38% | +40.1% | −7.5% | +10.8% | 10.7% | −26% |
| Without RS filter | 330 | 39% | +40.4% | −7.5% | +11.1% | 10.1% | −27% |
| Without not-extended filter | 326 | 37% | +41.5% | −7.6% | +10.8% | 10.2% | −27% |
| 1-year (52-week) high | 475 | 34% | +42.8% | −7.8% | +9.6% | 9.4% | −25% |
| 5-year high | 243 | 39% | +35.5% | −7.5% | +9.3% | 7.4% | −26% |
| **Old high ≥ 1 year old** | **38** | 45% | +47.5% | −7.2% | **+17.3%** | **3.2%** | **−10%** |
| Stop 15% | 286 | 47% | +41.7% | −9.1% | +14.9% | 11.3% | −25% |
| Trend exit 40 weeks | 300 | 37% | +52.1% | −8.0% | +14.0% | 11.1% | −29% |
| No trend exit (stop only) | 104 | 0%* | — | −8.5% | — | 10.5% | −30% |

\* Without a trend exit, winners are never sold, so only stopped-out losers "close". Its trade stats are meaningless; the portfolio just held its first ~33 winners.

**What it says (with the caveat that 11 variations on one survivorship-biased dataset can mislead):**
- **The filters barely matter.** Removing all three changed CAGR by under 1%/yr, in the "better" direction. RS and not-extended almost never block a trade. Volume removes about 25% of trades with almost no change in quality. The INFY anecdote (Day 1) didn't generalise. One example is a story, not evidence.
- **Lookback:** 3 years beat 1 and 5 years here. 1-year has more trades but a lower win rate, and 5-year has fewer opportunities so more idle cash.
- **"Old high ≥ 1 year old" gives the best trade quality** (+17.3% per trade, 45% win, −10% DD) **but is far too rare: 38 trades in 19 years**, so the portfolio sat in cash and made 3.2%/yr. It's a quality filter, not a whole system.
- **Exits matter more than entry filters.** A looser stop (15%) or a slower trend exit (40 weeks) each improved expectancy by about 3–4 points.
- **The robust result is risk:** every reasonable variation kept max drawdown at about −25% to −30% vs −60% for the index. That comes from the exits and cash, not from any filter.

## PKScreener / stock-pattern observations
**stock-pattern** (GPL-3.0, run on eod2 data, Python 3.12 via uv):
- Loads eod2 data correctly (checked HUDCO directly through its loader).
- **Detected nothing**: not on DIVISLAB, INFY, TCS, HEROMOTOCO or NAVINFLUOR just before their breakouts (daily or weekly), not on 20 large caps on 2024-04-25, and **not even its own README example** (HUDCO bullish VCP, 25 Apr 2024) on any day of April 2024. Stopped under the V0 time-box.
- Design notes: it looks for patterns *before* a breakout (the last leg), on the last ~160 bars only, with pivots 6 bars each side, and reports only on the exact day the last leg forms. Detection is very sensitive to dates and parameters, and when it finds nothing it gives no reason.
- **Lesson for Kanso:** pattern detectors that silently return nothing are hard to trust or debug. Kanso's Stock Report shows "not evaluable (reason)" and the evidence for every ✅/❌. Keep that.

**PKScreener** (Docker image `pkjmesra/pkscreener:latest`, v0.46.20260615):
- The image is **5.4 GB on disk** (1.3 GB compressed download) and needs Colima/Docker running.
- It runs unattended via menu codes (`-o X:<index>:<scan>`). Passing `-a Y` auto-answers prompts, which **also accepts its Disclaimer and Terms of Service**.
- **It now requires a login**: a user ID and one-time password from its Telegram bot (`@nse_pkscreener_bot`). It can't be used without a Telegram account.
- The market data bundled in the image was dated **2026-07-07**, about 4.5 months stale until it downloads fresh data.
- **Trial mode exists:** leave the Telegram User ID blank and choose "2 > I am a free/trial user". (Option 1 is a "Premium trial in Limited Mode".) The menus only render in an interactive terminal (`docker run -it`).
- **Scanner menu (Nifty 500): 49 scans**, mostly short-term indicator signals (RSI, MACD, CCI, Ichimoku, intraday). The ones relevant to Kanso: 17 "52 week high breakout (today)", 2 "Today's Breakouts", 1 "Probable Breakouts", 49 "52-week high approaching breakout", 4 "Lowest Volume in last N-days (Early Breakout Detection)", 3 "Consolidating stocks", 26 "Stocks with stock-split/bonus/dividends".
- **Scan 17 on Nifty 500 returned 0 results "in 0.1 sec" for 2026-11-20**, labelled "Already Cached", with "Queue Wait Time: 247s". The header still showed Nifty data from 2026-07-07. Zero stocks at a 52-week high across 500 on a normal day is implausible. It looks like trial mode served a cached or stale result instead of scanning fresh data. **Not trustworthy as a reference.**
- **Ideas for Kanso:** a "**approaching breakout**" watch list (stocks just under their multi-year high, like scan 49) is a natural V2 feature. Showing **when the data is from** on every result screen would have exposed PKScreener's stale data immediately. Kanso's honesty panel already does this (§5.8).
- **Lesson for Kanso:** a one-command local install with no account is a real advantage for a portfolio demo.

## Data: Yahoo vs eod2
**eod2_data snapshot (2026-11-14):** 3,780 NSE symbols, 570 MB, one CSV per symbol (`Date,Open,High,Low,Close,Volume,Series,TOTAL_TRADES,QTY_PER_TRADE,DLV_QTY`), BAJFINANCE from 2010. Extras Yahoo lacks: number of trades and **delivery quantity** (shares actually taken into demat, not traded intraday).

**Split/bonus check: BAJFINANCE, ex-date 2025-06-16** (a 1:2 split *and* a 4:1 bonus, 10× more shares in total)

| | eod2 | Yahoo |
|---|---|---|
| Prices before the ex-date | ✅ adjusted ÷10 (close 2025-06-13 = 933.10) | ✅ identical (933.10) |
| Volume before the ex-date | ❌ **not adjusted** (1,018,202) | ✅ adjusted ×10 (10,182,020) |
| Corporate-action record | none in the file | ⚠️ split event says **2:1** only, so the bonus is missing even though prices are adjusted ÷10 |

**Consequence:** with eod2's unadjusted volume, the 50-day volume ratio shows a **fake 6.2× "surge" on the ex-date and 9.4× four days later**, and stays distorted for about 50 trading days. Kanso's volume filter would fire falsely after every split or bonus. Yahoo's volume is correct here.

**Verdict for V1 (proposal):** keep **Yahoo** as the V1 source. If eod2 or bhavcopy is ever used, volume must be re-adjusted from a corporate-actions list. Never trust Yahoo's split *events* to reconstruct adjustments; use its already-adjusted bars (spec §6.1 already says so). Also: Yahoo dropped one request mid-session (RemoteDisconnected) and succeeded on retry, which confirms the retry/backoff requirement.

## Changes to make in the V1 spec
- **"Minimum age of the old high": add it as an optional filter (off by default), and always show the level's age as evidence.** Day 3 data: with a ≥ 1-year minimum, trades are the best quality (+17.3% each vs +11.1%) but only 38 in 19 years, so it isn't a good default. Showing "old high set N months ago" in the scanner and Stock Report is cheap and helps the user tell base breakouts from continuation highs.
- **Don't change the V1 defaults based on Day 3.** The differences between variations were small (about 1%/yr) on one survivorship-biased sample. The variations-tested counter and overfitting warning (§5.8) exist for exactly this.
- **Add a test: entry on a gap-up open still fills.** Size the position at the *fill* price (next open), never at the signal close. TradingView gets this wrong at 100% sizing (see above). The Kanso spec already sizes at the fill (§5.6), so this test pins that down.
- **Keep Yahoo as the V1 data source** (Day 2). eod2/bhavcopy prices are split-adjusted but volume isn't, which causes fake volume surges after splits and bonuses. Add a real-data test: BAJFINANCE's 50-day volume ratio on 2025-06-16 must be about 1×, not 6×.
- **V1 Task 13 (vectorbt benchmark): pass `open`, `high`, `low` to `from_signals`**, so stops are checked against the day's full range. Without them, vectorbt misses intraday stop hits and gap-downs (DIVISLAB 2020-07-02, Day 3). Also resolve the DIVISLAB 2025 stop-date disagreement there.
- **Honesty panel: show drawdown next to CAGR, and against the benchmark.** On Nifty 50 the system's edge in return was small (+0.7%/yr, likely inflated by survivorship), but its drawdown was far smaller (−27% vs −60%). Risk is half the story.
- **V2 idea: an "approaching breakout" watch list** of stocks within a few % of their multi-year high (PKScreener scan 49).
- **Ingest freshness:** Yahoo can lag a day behind the NSE close. The ingest must record the latest date it actually got, and the scanner must report "as of <date>" from the data, never from today's calendar date (Chartink comparison).
- **Note in the README:** after an exit, re-entry needs the rule to turn false and then true again (edge-triggered). If an exit happens while the price is still above the level, there's no re-entry until a fresh breakout.

## Lessons so far (domain)
- Stop-loss: INFY and TCS (2024 breakouts) lost −8.5% each under Kanso rules, against −42% for buy-and-hold.
- Breakout systems win less than half the time, but winners are about 2.5× losers (DIVISLAB: 45% win rate, +15.0% / −5.9%).
- Exits cost upside: DIVISLAB's Sept 2024 trade exited at +0.8% before the stock doubled. Re-entries (May 2025 −8.5%, Jul 2026 +52.7%) got some of it back: about +41% compounded vs +106% buy-and-hold.
- Volume matters: INFY's failed Sept 2024 breakout came on 0.73× average volume.
- (Day 2) Splits and bonuses rescale history. Prices *and* volume must both be adjusted, or indicators built on them lie (BAJFINANCE, June 2025).
- (Day 2) Any data provider can be wrong or stale: eod2's unadjusted volume, Yahoo's incomplete split record and dropped request, PKScreener's cached July data. Always check the data's date and spot-check against a second source.
- (Day 2) Pattern detectors that silently return nothing (stock-pattern) can't be trusted. Showing the evidence and the reason "why not" matters as much as showing matches.
