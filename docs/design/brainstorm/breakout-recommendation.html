My pick for a beginner: start simple, measure everything

You don't need to know which breakouts work. That's the backtester's job. Start with the ones a computer can detect without judgement, then let the data tell you which filters help.

Version 1: the core pattern

✓

New N-year high (A)

Why: it's one clear rule ("today's close is the highest in 3 years"), so there's nothing to argue about. Stocks making new long-term highs are also one of the most-studied effects in markets, so it's a credible place to start. Years is a dial you can change: 2, 3, 5, all-time.

Version 1: four trust filters (each one on/off in the builder)

1

Weekly close above the level

A stock that pokes above the high for an hour and falls back hasn't broken out. Waiting for the week to close above it filters out many fakes.

2

Volume surge

Volume = how many shares changed hands. A breakout on 2× normal volume means big buyers are involved; on thin volume it's often noise.

3

Beating the market

Is the stock up more than the Nifty over the last 6 months? Leaders tend to keep leading.

4

Not too far above the level

If it's already 25% above the old high, you're late and your stop-loss (exit if wrong) is far away. Within ~5–8% keeps the risk small.

Later (not version 1)

Add once V1 works

  • Tested ceiling (B) and long base (C): need "swing point" detection, the first genuinely hard algorithm. Great second milestone.
  • P&F resistance (D): comes for free once you build the P&F chart engine.

Skip for now

  • Tightening (VCP): experienced traders disagree on what it looks like, so it's hard to code fairly.
  • Sector breakouts: needs a reliable stock-to-sector map.
  • Fundamentals: needs a separate earnings data source.
